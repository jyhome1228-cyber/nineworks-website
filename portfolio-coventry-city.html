Project Overview

A crest for the next chapter.

Coventry City's existing crest carries much more than a football identity. It brings together civic symbols, historical memory and supporter recognition in one dense heraldic composition. The proposal begins from a simple position: that history should not be replaced simply to look modern. Instead, the inherited symbols can be reorganised into a clearer, more scalable and more consistent football brand system.

The project therefore focuses on structure rather than erasure. The visual memory of the club is retained, while drawing, proportion, line weight and colour hierarchy are refined so that the crest can work with equal confidence on a shirt, a phone screen, a broadcast graphic, a flag, a stadium façade or a piece of merchandise.

Why Now · The New Premier League Chapter

25 years away.
One new beginning.

The 2026/27 season marks Coventry City's return to the Premier League after a 25-year absence. That return creates a rare moment in which the club's identity is seen again at global scale.

A modern football crest no longer lives only on the left chest of a shirt. In the same week it can become a broadcast mark, an app icon, a social avatar, a retail object, a stadium sign and a supporter flag. Promotion therefore changes the context in which the identity has to perform.

This proposal asks one central question: can Coventry become clearer without becoming less Coventry? The answer proposed here is yes — by treating heritage as the content and design system as the method.

Coventry & The Sky Blues

A football identity inseparable from the city it represents.

Coventry City Football Club is visually inseparable from Coventry itself. The club's long-standing Sky Blue identity and the nickname “The Sky Blues” give it an immediate colour asset, while the crest carries symbols rooted in the city's civic heraldry. Together, colour and civic symbolism create an identity with a depth that many contemporary football brands have to invent from scratch.

That depth is exactly why the renewal should be careful. A fashionable badge can date quickly; a civic symbol carries accumulated meaning. The design direction therefore avoids turning Coventry into a generic modern football mark and instead asks how the existing memory can be made more legible, reproducible and consistent for a new audience and a new competitive context.

Civic Heritage

The badge carries the story of Coventry before it carries the story of football.

The existing crest is built from a rich collection of symbols derived from Coventry's civic identity. The elephant and castle are long-standing city emblems; the eagle and phoenix connect the badge to a wider story of ancient Coventry, destruction, recovery and renewal. The football and club name then bring that civic narrative into the sporting context.

The proposal does not flatten these elements into a single generic icon. Instead, it treats each symbol as a module within one stronger composition. The central question becomes not “what can be deleted?” but “what must remain, and how can it be read more clearly?”

Identity Challenge

Heritage is powerful. Complexity is not the same thing.

The original crest has a strong symbolic foundation, but its detailed illustration, multiple competing elements and broad colour range can become difficult to control across modern brand applications. At small scale, details can merge. In embroidery, fine line work can change character. In social and broadcast environments, the overall silhouette has to register before a viewer has time to read every symbol.

The response is not radical simplification. It is hierarchy. The proposal uses a stronger circular badge architecture, controlled detail, more consistent line relationships and a reduced primary colour system so that meaning remains present while recognition becomes faster.

SMALL SCALERecognition

The crest should remain identifiable when reduced to a social avatar or score graphic.

PRODUCTIONReproducibility

Embroidery, print, patch and one-colour production should all feel intentional.

DIGITALAvatar Ready

A circular structure naturally adapts to app icons, thumbnails and profile formats.

BRANDOne Visual Voice

Kit, stadium, social and merchandise should feel like one club rather than separate executions.

Crest Renewal

From an illustrative heraldic crest to a structured football badge.

The renewed direction transforms the existing composition into a modern circular badge without abandoning its symbolic language. A bold navy outer ring creates a more stable silhouette and frames the club name as part of the mark. “Coventry City” sits along the upper curve and “Football Club” along the lower curve, allowing the name and civic symbols to work as one lock-up.

At the centre, the elephant and three towers remain the primary heritage anchor. The eagle and phoenix continue to support the narrative on either side, while the football and simplified flame resolve the lower area. The difference between before and after is therefore not the deletion of history, but the creation of a clearer visual order.

Color System

Sky Blue is not decoration. It is recognition.

The renewed palette clarifies the relationship between colours rather than allowing every colour to compete at the same level. Sky Blue becomes the emotional signature and most immediate recognition cue. Navy provides structure, contrast and authority. White creates breathing room, while red is reserved for controlled symbolic accents such as the cross and flame.

This hierarchy allows the crest to move more easily between full-colour, monochrome and reversed applications while preserving a consistent club identity across different materials and production methods.

Branding Applications

Not just a badge. A club-wide identity tool.

The proposal is tested as a practical football brand asset rather than a logo shown only on a presentation board. The stronger silhouette, simplified internal relationships and Sky Blue / Navy hierarchy are intended to remain recognisable across fabric, print, stadium-scale graphics, supporter items, merchandise and digital content.

The application studies show how the system can shift in scale without changing personality. A chest badge requires precision. A supporter banner requires distance recognition. A stadium environment requires strong fields of colour. A digital icon requires immediate silhouette. The same identity has to survive all of them.

Final Statement

Built from Coventry. Ready for the modern game.

This proposal is not about replacing Coventry City's past with something new. It is about organising what already matters into a system that can be used longer, seen more clearly and applied more consistently. Heritage remains the centre; clarity, scalability and contemporary usability make it ready for the next chapter.
